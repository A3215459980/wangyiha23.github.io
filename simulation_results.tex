\subsection{Simulation Results}

Fig.~\ref{fig:ser16} and Fig.~\ref{fig:ser32} compare the symbol error
rate (SER) performance of different detectors under 64-QAM modulation
for the \mbox{$N_t\!=\!N_r\!=\!16$} and
\mbox{$N_t\!=\!N_r\!=\!32$} systems, respectively. The two figures show
a consistent trend: the SER of all detectors decreases as the SNR
increases, while the performance gaps among the detectors become more
pronounced in the high-SNR region. Among the classical baselines, MMSE
performs the worst, OAMP improves the result through iterative
decoupling, and EP further benefits from posterior refinement. The
learning-aided detectors achieve lower SER than the classical methods,
where GEPNet and GCEPNet improve EP by exploiting correlation
information, and DEPNet gives the best performance over the whole SNR
range. In particular, for \mbox{$N_t\!=\!N_r\!=\!32$}, DEPNet achieves
about $1$\,dB gain over GCEPNet at SER\,$=10^{-4}$, which demonstrates
the benefit of tracking the evolving EP belief with the sparse coupling
graph, content-adaptive attention, and cross-attention.
